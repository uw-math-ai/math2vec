For any generator $p = (a, b)$ in $\alpha \times \text{Bool}$, there is no one-step reduction from the singleton word $[p]$ to any other word $L$ in the free group construction.
The multiplicative equivalence (group isomorphism) between a free group $G$ and the free group generated by its set of generators $\text{Generators}(G)$. This isomorphism demonstrates that any free group is isomorphic to the free group constructed from its generators.
Given a group isomorphism $e \colon G \cong H$, if $G$ is a free group, then $H$ is also a free group.
For any function $f \colon \alpha \to \beta$ and any element $x$ in the free group over $\alpha$, the homomorphism $\text{map}\ f$ applied to $x$ equals the homomorphism $\text{lift}\ (\text{of} \circ f)$ applied to $x$. That is, $\text{map}\ f\ x = \text{lift}\ (\text{of} \circ f)\ x$.
For any two lists $L_1$ and $L_2$ representing words in the free group, if $L_1$ reduces to $L_2$ under the reflexive-transitive closure of the one-step reduction relation, then the length of $L_2$ is less than or equal to the length of $L_1$.
For any generator $x$ in the free group construction, the word $[x]$ reduces to another word $L₁$ if and only if $L₁$ is equal to $[x]$.
For any words $L_1, L_2, L_3$ in the free group (represented as lists of pairs $(a, b)$ where $a$ is a generator and $b$ indicates inversion), if $L_1$ reduces to $L_2$ and $L_1$ reduces to $L_3$ under the reflexive-transitive closure of the free group reduction relation, then there exists a word $L_4$ such that both $L_2$ and $L_3$ reduce to $L_4$.
Given a free group basis $b \colon \iota \to G$ for a group $G$, the group $G$ is a free group. That is, there exists an isomorphism between $G$ and the free group generated by $\iota$.
Given a word $L₁$ in the free group represented as a list of pairs $(a, b)$ where $a$ is a generator and $b$ indicates inversion, the function returns the list of all words $L₂$ that can be obtained by reducing $L₁$ through cancellation of adjacent inverses. Specifically, it filters the sublists of $L₁$ to those that are related to $L₁$ by the reflexive-transitive closure of the one-step reduction relation.
For any two words $L₁$ and $L₂$ in a free group, if $L₁$ reduces to $L₂$ (denoted $L₁ \sim L₂$), then $L₁$ also reduces to the maximal reduction of $L₂$ (denoted $L₁ \sim \mathrm{reduce}(L₂)$).
